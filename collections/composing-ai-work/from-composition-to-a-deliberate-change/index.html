From composition to a deliberate change

Application units, the execution record and a version change chosen on purpose.

A purchasing assistant has extracted an agreement's payment terms and is waiting for a delivery date. Someone can supply the date, and a reviewer can check the terms, before the rest of the work continues. YojnAI's composition model gives that contribution a place: an intermediate result, a check and an application that can resume when the missing context arrives. The guide to the purchase request sets out the example; the first article here develops it.

Suppose the completed report is still wrong, because the reviewer disputes a payment term. The composition identifies work we can investigate; its execution record helps follow what happened. A faulty extraction, a bad lookup and a correction lost later point towards different remedies. The investigation can now choose a part to change rather than begin again from the final answer.

The remedy encounters the rest of the application. A changed lookup may solve this case while confusing another supplier. The team needs to bring both cases to the candidate, see which part improved and decide whether the trade-off fits the operation. The next version then carries a particular choice: a changed source-selection rule, for example, with evidence for retaining it. The purchase gives somebody a place to contribute; investigating that contribution gives the application a way to improve beyond this one purchase.
