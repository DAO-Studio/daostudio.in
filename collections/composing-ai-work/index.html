Composing AI work

The context, traces, feedback and versions around a model-driven application.

An application can be useful before it has a final answer. Someone can review the terms already extracted from an agreement while another person supplies the date still missing from a purchase. Work has begun, and people have something meaningful to contribute. Waiting for one finished response would hide that opportunity.

YojnAI brings this question into a platform for composing, deploying, inspecting and improving AI applications. It reached pilots in investment banking, operations and the CFO office; its wider ambition is to make this developing work available to the people and systems around it. The interesting unit is not merely a model capable of answering. It is a contribution another person or component could use, question and change without losing the rest of the operation. That asks for a place to enter, an account of what has already happened and a way to see which later work depends on a correction.

There is a tension here. Structure can make a correction precise, but too much structure can make every exception a programming task. A business operator needs to be able to say why a proposed answer does not fit their work; an engineer needs enough of the path to investigate it. A natural conversation can invite that contribution while a more explicit account keeps it consequential after the conversation ends. Neither can do the other's whole job.

The ambition is an application people can help become better through ordinary use. A correction should reach its source or mechanism, a useful improvement should survive the next request, and the business should be able to recognise what changed. Making that possible is part of the application itself, not a separate appeal for feedback after it has finished working.
