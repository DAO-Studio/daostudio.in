Title: Coordinating emergencies

[Read HTML](https://daostudio.in/collections/coordinating-emergencies/)

Author: Harsh Joshi

Someone asks for help. The service receives the request, but a nearby contact is unavailable and the image sent to a reviewer is old. There is already something to handle. The next person needs a way to carry it without pretending that the missing part has been settled.

[YOJN Care](https://daostudio.in/yojn-care/) brings that continuity into an emergency-response product for homes, shops and neighbourhoods, connecting personal SOS, monitored sites, human review and intended response. A deliberate request and a camera observation can begin different work. People need enough evidence to judge the situation, enough room to correct an interpretation and an intelligible commitment when the incident passes to somebody else. The incident keeps changing while those contributions travel.

The question is how the parts can support one another. A clearer view may save a reviewer from a repeated call. A preserved question may let the next shift continue rather than start again. Grouping observations may reduce duplicate work while keeping a meaningful change visible. The gains depend on a service able to receive them: somebody with time to review, equipment that continues to supply useful evidence and a response arrangement people can actually carry.

That makes the unfinished case important. It still holds a question, consumes some continuing capacity and may reveal where the service cannot proceed. Keeping it intelligible helps the operation improve. Making it disappear from the report helps only the report.

One way to read the essays is to follow a single incident. [The evidence path](https://daostudio.in/collections/coordinating-emergencies/the-evidence-path/) asks what reaches the reviewer, [An interface with room to act](https://daostudio.in/collections/coordinating-emergencies/an-interface-with-room-to-act/) what people can do with it, and [Who carries the incident?](https://daostudio.in/collections/coordinating-emergencies/who-carries-the-incident/) how the work passes between them. [The cases that are still waiting](https://daostudio.in/collections/coordinating-emergencies/the-cases-that-are-still-waiting/) asks what an unfinished case means for the results, and [The cost of continuing response](https://daostudio.in/collections/coordinating-emergencies/the-cost-of-continuing-response/) what keeping the service going takes.
