Title: A court before the consequence

[Read HTML](https://daostudio.in/collections/protocols-and-reuse/a-court-before-the-consequence/)

Author: Harsh Joshi

An AI that could rewrite its own safeguards prompted the thought experiment of a precognition court: put the rules outside the actor’s unilateral control, and make an action pass their judgment before it happens. The proposal brings two problems together. People need a way to govern and improve the rules, and the machinery carrying the action needs to respect the decision.

A document service makes their connection visible. An objection to a report asks who can examine the rule, hear the challenge and correct a mistake. A change to the approved report then asks which version may be released and what happens if another route can bypass the decision. The institution and the execution path need each other, but neither can supply the other's answer.

A common ledger is interesting within that relationship: participants could coordinate an exchange or a review without giving one organisation sole control of its only account. The protocol ambition was useful shared action, with a record the people depending on it could inspect and contest. Its value depends on the judgment and consequence it helps connect. The wider possibility is an arrangement people can question, correct and rely on, rather than merely a durable record of whatever the actor chose to do.
