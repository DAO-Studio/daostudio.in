Review an incident and carry it onward

YOJN Care

YOJN Care is not a live emergency or dispatch service; this guide explains its monitoring workflow. If you need help now, call your local emergency number.

You take over a shop incident from another operator. Two camera views are attached, the owner has been called and a reply is still awaited. Can you continue from there, or must you investigate everything again? Care brings the site's instructions, images, personal requests and earlier review together so the next person can see what happened and what remains to do.

Begin with the evidence actually available

A person appears in the shop during its unattended hours. The owner may recognise authorised staff; an operator may need a reference view or the owner's explanation before deciding what the image means. The detection begins that review.

Related frames and notifications belong together. One camera may show something another misses, and a later frame may change the interpretation. A judgment on one image should not close every observation in the incident.

A deliberate SOS has a different starting point: somebody asked for help. It can need attention even when no camera shows the situation. The reviewer needs to recognise that origin rather than see every alert as another camera-detection task.

Check whether the view can support the question

Suppose the shop camera appears connected, but its picture is old. It cannot tell you whether someone is still inside. You need a current view or an answer from the owner before drawing that conclusion.

Capture time and upload time therefore matter separately. So do unattended hours, camera placement, contact details and the site's instructions. Missing or unusable evidence needs to be apparent. The evidence path explains how keeping those inputs usable sustains the coverage.

Decide what the image means, then carry the answer onward

The review compares the frame with the reference view and the owner's instructions, distinguishing crime, suspicion, non-suspicion and a false positive. Selecting an answer and forwarding it are separate actions: the reviewer can reset an answer or leave it incomplete. Using the siren or speaker is separate too, with one person in control at a time.

A case cannot wait forever for somebody to pick it up. Care's automatic-detection workflow gives the operator a pickup allowance, then passes an unattended case to the manager and, after another timeout, to the community as suspicious. Loading evidence and calling the owner can extend handling time. The state and clock example sets out the allowances. They concern review, not the time for help to arrive.

Tell the next operator what still needs doing

In the shop incident, “owner called” is not enough. The handover needs to say that the reply is still awaited, which two views were examined and whether more camera notifications have arrived. The new operator can then continue without repeating the investigation or assuming the owner has answered.

The handover also needs someone who agrees to continue. A community contact may be unavailable, and an operator who can review images may have no authority to dispatch help. Opening an alert establishes neither commitment.

An authorised responder may act outside the interface. Without their update, the outcome remains uncertain. Closing a shift or moving the case to another queue should not turn that uncertainty into a completed response. Who carries the incident? develops this responsibility; Follow an incident connects the review to the personal application and the service arrangements it needs.
